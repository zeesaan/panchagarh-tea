How to Make the Perfect Cup of Black Tea

A simple guide to brewing rich, smooth and enjoyable black tea at home.

How Do You Make a Good Cup of Black Tea?

Making a great cup of black tea does not need to be complicated. Good tea, fresh water, the right amount of leaves and a suitable brewing time can make a noticeable difference.

The ideal method can vary depending on the type and size of the tea leaves. A simple starting point is to use hot water and adjust the brewing time according to your preferred strength.

Simple starting method: Use about 2–3 grams of black tea for 250 ml of water and steep for around 3–4 minutes. Adjust the time and tea quantity to taste.

What You Need

Simple Black Tea Recipe

  • Black tea: 2–3 grams
  • Water: 250 ml
  • Water temperature: Around 90–100°C
  • Brewing time: Around 3–4 minutes
  • Sugar: Optional
  • Milk: Optional

Step 1: Start With Fresh Water

Start with fresh, clean water. Water makes up most of the finished cup, so its quality can have a noticeable effect on the taste.

Bring the water to a suitable temperature for the black tea you are using.

Step 2: Measure Your Tea

For a standard 250 ml cup, start with approximately 2–3 grams of loose black tea.

If you prefer a stronger cup, you can increase the amount of tea slightly instead of making the brewing time much longer.

Tea tip:

Different black teas can require different brewing parameters. Whole-leaf teas and smaller broken leaves may release flavour at different rates, so use the suggested amount as a starting point.

Step 3: Add Hot Water

Pour hot water over the tea leaves.

For a straightforward Western-style cup, water around 90–100°C is a useful starting range for many black teas.

Some delicate black teas are better brewed below boiling. The exact temperature therefore depends on the tea.

Step 4: Let the Tea Steep

Allow the tea to steep for around 3–4 minutes as a starting point.

Shorter brewing can produce a lighter cup, while longer brewing generally produces a stronger and more intense infusion.

If the tea becomes too strong or bitter for your taste, reduce the brewing time rather than continuing to steep the same leaves.

Step 5: Remove the Tea Leaves

Once the tea reaches your preferred strength, remove the leaves or strain the tea.

Do not leave the leaves sitting in the hot water indefinitely, because the flavour will continue to develop.

Should You Add Milk?

Yes, if you enjoy milk tea.

Black tea can be enjoyed plain or with milk. Milk is particularly common in many South Asian tea traditions.

For a simple milk tea, brew the black tea first and then add a small amount of warm milk according to your preferred taste.

For a stronger milk tea:

Use a slightly stronger tea infusion before adding milk so that the tea flavour remains noticeable.

Should You Add Sugar?

Sugar is completely optional.

If you enjoy sweet tea, add sugar after brewing and stir until it dissolves.

For a more natural tea experience, try the tea without sugar first. This allows you to notice the tea's aroma, strength and flavour.

Why Does Black Tea Become Bitter?

A cup of black tea can taste too bitter or astringent when it is brewed too strongly for the particular tea.

Common things to adjust include the amount of tea, water temperature and brewing time.

  • Use less tea.
  • Reduce the brewing time.
  • Try slightly cooler water for delicate black tea.
  • Use fresh water.

How to Make Strong Black Tea

If you prefer a strong cup, start by slightly increasing the amount of tea.

You can also adjust the brewing time, but avoid making large changes all at once.

A small adjustment allows you to find the balance between strength, aroma and smoothness that you prefer.

How to Make Black Tea Without Milk

For plain black tea, simply brew the tea leaves in hot water and strain after the desired brewing time.

This method lets the natural colour and flavour of the tea come through without milk or sugar.

Loose Leaf Tea vs Tea Bags

Both loose-leaf tea and tea bags can make a good cup of black tea.

Loose-leaf tea gives you more control over the amount of tea and allows the leaves more room to expand during brewing.

Tea bags are convenient and easy to use, especially when you want to prepare one cup quickly.

Can You Reuse Black Tea Leaves?

Whether tea leaves can be reused depends on the type and size of the leaves and the brewing method.

Larger whole leaves may retain flavour for additional infusions, while smaller or broken leaves can release much of their flavour during the first brew.

If you want to reuse leaves, it is better to brew them again soon rather than leaving wet leaves around for an extended period.

How to Make Black Tea Taste Better

If your tea does not taste right, change one factor at a time.

  • Try better quality tea leaves.
  • Use fresh water.
  • Measure the tea consistently.
  • Control the brewing time.
  • Experiment with water temperature.
  • Try the tea without sugar or milk first.

A Simple Panchagarh Black Tea Method

Everyday Panchagarh Black Tea

  1. Boil approximately 250 ml of fresh water.
  2. Add around 2–3 grams of black tea.
  3. Pour the hot water over the tea.
  4. Steep for approximately 3–4 minutes.
  5. Strain the tea.
  6. Add sugar or milk if you prefer.
  7. Serve hot and enjoy.

The Most Important Tip

There is no single brewing method that tastes perfect to everyone.

Start with a simple recipe and adjust it based on the tea you are using and your personal preference.

If the cup is too weak, use a little more tea or steep slightly longer. If it is too strong, reduce the amount of tea or shorten the brewing time.

Enjoy Your Cup of Panchagarh Tea

Good tea does not need a complicated process.

With fresh water, quality black tea and a little attention to brewing time, you can make a satisfying cup at home.

Try different brewing times and tea quantities until you find the flavour you enjoy most.

And when you are ready, enjoy a cup of authentic Panchagarh Tea.
