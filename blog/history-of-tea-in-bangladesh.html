History of Tea in Bangladesh

From the early tea gardens of Chattogram and Sylhet to the growing tea region of Panchagarh.

Home   /   Blog   /   History of Tea in Bangladesh

How Did Tea Begin in Bangladesh?

Tea has a long history in Bangladesh. The development of tea cultivation began during the British period, first in the eastern parts of the region and later expanding into other parts of the country.

Today, Bangladesh has tea gardens in several regions, including the traditional tea-growing areas of Sylhet and Chattogram and the newer tea-growing region of northern Bangladesh.

A short history: Experimental tea cultivation began in Chattogram in 1840, commercial cultivation began at Malnichhera Tea Garden in Sylhet in 1854, and small-holding tea cultivation around Tetulia, Panchagarh, developed much later, beginning around 2000.

The Beginning of Tea Cultivation in Chattogram

According to Bangladesh Tea Board material, tea cultivation was first attempted experimentally in the Pioneer Garden area of Chattogram in 1840.

Tea seeds from Assam and Chinese tea plants were used in these early experiments.

These experiments were an important early step in the development of tea cultivation in what is now Bangladesh.

Commercial Tea Cultivation in Sylhet

The commercial development of tea became more established in Sylhet during the nineteenth century.

Bangladesh Tea Board records identify 1854 as the year commercial tea cultivation began at Malnichhera Tea Garden in Sylhet.

Sylhet and the surrounding areas eventually became the heart of Bangladesh's traditional tea industry.

Why Did Tea Grow in Sylhet?

The Sylhet region became strongly associated with tea because of its natural environment and suitability for tea cultivation.

Over time, large tea estates developed across Sylhet and the surrounding tea-growing areas.

Tea cultivation became an important agricultural and economic activity in the region.

Tea Expansion During the British Period

Tea cultivation expanded during the British period and became an important plantation industry.

Large estates, processing facilities and supporting infrastructure developed around the major tea-growing regions.

The industry also created a large workforce connected with tea gardens, processing and related activities.

Tea in Bangladesh After Independence

After Bangladesh became independent in 1971, the tea industry entered a new period of development.

The government worked to rehabilitate tea gardens affected by the Liberation War and to support the continued development of the industry.

Bangladesh Tea Board records also describe efforts to improve tea research, infrastructure, machinery and worker welfare during the early years after independence.

Bangladesh Tea Research

Tea research has played an important role in improving cultivation and production in Bangladesh.

The Bangladesh Tea Research Institute, commonly known as BTRI, became a full-fledged research institute in 1973.

Research supports areas such as tea varieties, cultivation practices, production and quality improvement.

Important Milestones in Bangladesh Tea History

1840 — Early Tea Experiment

Experimental tea cultivation was started in the Pioneer Garden area of Chattogram.

1854 — Commercial Tea in Sylhet

Commercial tea cultivation began at Malnichhera Tea Garden in Sylhet.

1971 — Independence of Bangladesh

The tea industry entered a new period following the independence of Bangladesh.

1973 — Bangladesh Tea Research Institute

The Bangladesh Tea Research Station was upgraded to a full-fledged Bangladesh Tea Research Institute.

Around 2000 — Panchagarh Tea

Small-holding tea cultivation began developing around Tetulia in Panchagarh, creating a new tea-growing region in northern Bangladesh.

The Development of Tea in Panchagarh

Panchagarh represents a newer chapter in the history of tea in Bangladesh.

Unlike the traditional tea estates of Sylhet and Chattogram, northern Bangladesh developed a significant small-holding tea sector.

A Bangladesh Tea Research Institute journal describes the beginning of small-holding tea cultivation around Tetulia in Panchagarh in 2000.

The same research describes the soil and climatic conditions of the region as favourable for tea cultivation.

Why Is Panchagarh Important to Modern Tea?

Panchagarh expanded the geographical reach of tea cultivation in Bangladesh.

The development of tea gardens and small tea holdings around Tetulia created a distinctive tea landscape in the northern part of the country.

The region is now closely associated with tea gardens, tea growers and the growing tea economy of northern Bangladesh.

Did you know?

Panchagarh's tea story is relatively recent compared with the nineteenth-century tea estates of Sylhet and Chattogram. Its small-holding tea sector began developing around Tetulia around 2000.

Small Tea Growers in Panchagarh

One of the distinctive features of Panchagarh's tea industry is the role of small growers.

Instead of relying only on large plantation estates, tea cultivation expanded through smaller holdings across the northern tea-growing area.

This created a different model of tea cultivation and connected more local agricultural land and growers with the tea industry.

From Tea Garden to Tea Factory

Tea leaves harvested from tea gardens must go through processing before becoming the finished tea used by consumers.

Depending on the type of tea being produced, processing can include steps such as withering, rolling, oxidation, drying and sorting.

These processes help determine the appearance, aroma and flavour of the final tea.

Black Tea and Green Tea in Bangladesh

Black tea has traditionally been an important part of Bangladesh's tea industry and tea-drinking culture.

Green tea is also produced and consumed in Bangladesh, offering a different flavour and processing style.

The two teas come from the same tea plant but are processed differently, which creates their distinct characteristics.

The Bangladesh Tea Board

The Bangladesh Tea Board is an important institution in the country's tea sector.

Its work is connected with tea development, regulation, research, production and other aspects of the industry.

The development of national tea institutions has helped support Bangladesh's tea industry as it has expanded and changed over time.

Tea and Everyday Life in Bangladesh

Tea is more than an agricultural product in Bangladesh.

It is part of everyday social life, from a simple cup at home to tea served to guests, conversations at local tea stalls and tea enjoyed during work breaks.

This everyday connection has helped tea become one of the most familiar drinks in the country.

The Growth of Panchagarh Tea

The development of Panchagarh tea shows how Bangladesh's tea industry has continued to evolve beyond its traditional regions.

The tea gardens around Tetulia have become part of the identity and landscape of northern Bangladesh.

Tea cultivation has also created a connection between agriculture, local communities and tourism in Panchagarh.

Tea Gardens and Tourism

Tea gardens have become one of the attractions for people visiting Panchagarh.

Visitors can explore the green tea landscape around Tetulia and combine a tea garden trip with other destinations in the district.

These include the Mahananda River, Banglabandha, historical sites and viewpoints associated with the region.

Tea and the Identity of Panchagarh

Tea has become an important part of Panchagarh's modern identity.

The region's tea gardens are different from the traditional plantation landscapes of Sylhet, giving northern Bangladesh its own tea story.

For visitors and tea lovers, Panchagarh offers the opportunity to experience tea not only as a drink but also as part of the landscape and culture of northern Bangladesh.

The Future of Tea in Bangladesh

Bangladesh's tea industry continues to develop through research, improved cultivation practices, new tea-growing areas and changing consumer preferences.

The growth of Panchagarh demonstrates that the country's tea story is not limited to its traditional tea regions.

Northern Bangladesh now has its own important place in that story.

From Sylhet to Panchagarh: The history of tea in Bangladesh spans more than a century and continues to evolve through new growing regions, research, growers and changing tea culture.
