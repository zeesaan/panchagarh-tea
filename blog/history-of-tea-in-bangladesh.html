From the early tea gardens of Chattogram and Sylhet to the growing tea gardens of Panchagarh.

How Did Tea Begin in Bangladesh?

Tea has a long history in the region that is now Bangladesh. The development of commercial tea cultivation began during the nineteenth century, first in Chattogram and later in Sylhet.

Over time, tea cultivation expanded into one of Bangladesh's important agricultural industries. Today, tea is grown not only in the traditional tea-growing areas of Sylhet and Chattogram, but also in northern districts including Panchagarh.

Bangladesh Tea Board records the beginning of experimental tea cultivation in Chattogram in the early 1840s, followed by commercial tea cultivation at Malnichhara in Sylhet in 1854.

The Beginning of Tea Cultivation in Chattogram

One of the earliest chapters of Bangladesh's tea history is connected with Chattogram.

During the British period, tea plants were experimentally cultivated in the Chattogram area. Bangladesh Tea Board publications identify the Pioneer Garden in Chattogram as an important early location for experimental tea cultivation around 1840.

These early experiments helped demonstrate that tea could be cultivated successfully in the region.

Malnichhara and the Growth of Tea in Sylhet

Sylhet became one of the most important centres of tea cultivation in the region during the nineteenth century.

Malnichhara Tea Garden in Sylhet was established in 1854 and is widely recognized as the first commercial tea garden in Bangladesh.

From Sylhet, tea cultivation expanded into surrounding areas. The region eventually became one of the country's most important tea-producing landscapes.

Tea Expansion During the British Period

As commercial tea production developed, more tea gardens were established in Sylhet and Chattogram.

Tea became an important plantation crop and an increasingly organized industry. Processing, transportation, trading and auction systems developed alongside tea cultivation.

The history of Bangladesh's tea industry is therefore closely connected with the economic and agricultural history of the region during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

Tea in Bangladesh After Independence

After Bangladesh became independent in 1971, the tea industry entered a new period of development.

The government worked to rehabilitate tea gardens affected by the Liberation War and to support production, research and infrastructure.

The Bangladesh Tea Board continues to oversee and support development of the country's tea industry, including production, marketing, exports, new tea gardens and rehabilitation of abandoned gardens.

Important Milestones in Bangladesh Tea History

1840 — Early experimental cultivation

Tea was experimentally cultivated in the Chattogram area, including the Pioneer Garden.

1854 — Malnichhara

Commercial tea cultivation began at Malnichhara Tea Garden in Sylhet.

1951 — Tea Board

The Pakistan Tea Board was established under the Pakistan Tea Act of 1950.

1971 — Independence of Bangladesh

Bangladesh's tea industry entered a new national phase after independence.

1977 — Bangladesh Tea Board

The Bangladesh Tea Board was established under the Tea Ordinance of 1977.

2000 — Panchagarh

Tea cultivation was introduced in northern Bangladesh, particularly around Tetulia in Panchagarh.

The Development of Tea in Panchagarh

The development of tea cultivation in Panchagarh created a new chapter in the history of Bangladeshi tea.

According to Bangladesh Tea Board's Panchagarh information, surveys were carried out in Panchagarh and Thakurgaon in 1999 to assess the potential for tea cultivation in northern Bangladesh.

Small-scale tea cultivation subsequently developed around Tetulia, and the region gradually became an important new tea-growing area.

Bangladesh Tea Research Institute research also describes the introduction of tea plantation in northern Bangladesh, particularly Panchagarh, around 2000.

Why Is Panchagarh Important to Modern Tea?

Panchagarh is different from the traditional hill-based tea landscapes of Sylhet and Chattogram because much of its tea cultivation developed on relatively flat land.

Small tea growers and tea gardens became an important part of the tea landscape around Tetulia and other parts of Panchagarh.

This expansion helped establish tea cultivation as an important agricultural activity in northern Bangladesh.

From Traditional Black Tea to New Tea Varieties

Bangladesh has a long tradition of producing black tea. However, the country's tea industry has also expanded into other varieties.

Bangladesh Tea Association notes that producers have increasingly developed green, oolong and organic teas in addition to the traditional black tea produced in the country.

This gives Bangladeshi tea producers opportunities to serve different preferences and markets.

The Bangladesh Tea Board

The Bangladesh Tea Board plays an important role in the country's tea industry.

Its responsibilities include supporting tea production, marketing and exports, establishing new tea gardens, rehabilitating abandoned gardens and helping regulate the overall tea industry.

The Bangladesh Tea Board currently operates under the Tea Act, 2016.

Tea Research in Bangladesh

Research has also played an important role in the development of Bangladeshi tea.

The Bangladesh Tea Research Institute, commonly known as BTRI, conducts research related to tea productivity, quality, cultivation techniques, pests, diseases and tea processing.

Scientific research helps tea growers improve production while adapting cultivation methods to different growing regions.

Tea and Everyday Life in Bangladesh

Tea has become much more than an agricultural product in Bangladesh.

A cup of tea is part of everyday life for many people. Tea is commonly enjoyed at home, at work, in restaurants and during social conversations.

From a simple cup of black tea to green tea and other varieties, tea has become closely connected with Bangladeshi food culture and hospitality.

The Future of Panchagarh Tea

The expansion of tea cultivation in Panchagarh shows how the geography of Bangladesh's tea industry has changed over time.

The northern tea-growing region now forms an important part of Bangladesh's wider tea story, connecting traditional tea culture with newer growing areas and small-scale tea cultivation.

For Panchagarh, tea is now closely connected with agriculture, local livelihoods, tourism and the identity of the region.

Discover the Story Behind Panchagarh Tea

The history of tea in Bangladesh began more than a century ago, but the story continues to develop.

From the early gardens of Chattogram and Sylhet to the tea fields of Tetulia and Panchagarh, every growing region has contributed to the country's tea culture.

If you visit Panchagarh, take some time to explore the tea gardens and experience the landscape where one of Bangladesh's newest major tea-growing regions developed.
