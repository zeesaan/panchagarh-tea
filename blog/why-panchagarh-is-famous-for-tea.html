Why Is Panchagarh Famous for Tea?

Panchagarh has become well known for tea because tea cultivation developed successfully in the northern part of Bangladesh, particularly around Tetulia.

The region has soil and climatic conditions that can support tea cultivation. Over time, tea gardens and small tea holdings expanded, creating a distinctive tea-growing landscape in northern Bangladesh.

Panchagarh's tea story is closely connected with Tetulia, where small-holding tea cultivation began around 2000 and gradually expanded across the northern tea-growing region.

1. Panchagarh Has Suitable Soil for Tea

One of the important reasons tea cultivation developed in Panchagarh is the suitability of the local soil.

Bangladesh Tea Research Institute research describes the soil of the region as suitable for tea, including sandy-loam soil and an acidic soil pH that can support tea cultivation.

These natural conditions helped growers establish tea plantations successfully in an area that was not traditionally known as a major tea-growing region.

2. The Climate Supports Tea Cultivation

Panchagarh is located near the Himalayan foothills. The region has seasonal temperature and rainfall patterns that can support tea cultivation.

At the same time, tea growers have to manage periods of high temperature and dry conditions. Irrigation and shade management are therefore important for maintaining healthy tea plants.

Tea and the Himalayan Foothills

Panchagarh's location near the Himalayan foothills gives the region a different environmental setting from Bangladesh's traditional tea-growing areas.

3. Tea Cultivation Grew Around Tetulia

Tetulia is at the centre of Panchagarh's tea story.

Bangladesh Tea Research Institute research records a humble beginning of tea cultivation in northern Bangladesh around 2000. The development of small tea holdings at Tetulia helped establish tea cultivation as an important agricultural activity in the area.

Since then, the number and area of tea holdings in northern Bangladesh have increased.

4. Small Tea Growers Are Important

Panchagarh's tea landscape is not made up only of large traditional tea estates. Small growers have also played an important role in the development of tea cultivation in the region.

Bangladesh Tea Board material has highlighted the development of small-holding tea cultivation in northern Bangladesh.

This model has allowed tea cultivation to spread across agricultural land and has created a different pattern of tea production compared with the large plantation estates traditionally associated with Sylhet and Chattogram.

5. Panchagarh Added a New Dimension to Bangladesh Tea

For many years, Bangladesh's tea industry was strongly associated with Sylhet and Chattogram.

The development of tea cultivation in northern Bangladesh expanded the geographic range of the country's tea industry.

Bangladesh Tea Board describes plain-land tea cultivation in the northern part of the country as a new dimension in Bangladesh's tea industry.

6. Panchagarh Tea Has a Modern Production Story

Tea cultivation in northern Bangladesh developed relatively recently compared with the country's historic tea estates.

Because the region is newer to commercial tea cultivation, growers have adopted modern planting materials and cultivation techniques.

Bangladesh Tea Research Institute research reported that most tea in northern Bangladesh was planted using clonal planting materials.

7. The Tea Industry Is Growing

The northern tea-growing region has become an increasingly important part of Bangladesh's tea sector.

Bangladesh Tea Board reported that tea production in the northern plain-land region increased substantially during the years covered by its recent industry review.

The Tea Board also reported the successful completion of tea extension projects in Panchagarh and Lalmonirhat in 2023.

8. Panchagarh Has Its Own Tea Landscape

Tea gardens in Panchagarh have a distinctive landscape.

Instead of only large estate-style plantations, visitors can see tea cultivation spread across the countryside, particularly around Tetulia and nearby areas.

The green tea fields combine naturally with the open landscape of northern Bangladesh.

9. Tea and Tourism Are Connected

Panchagarh's tea gardens are also connected with the growing interest in tourism in the district.

Visitors can combine tea garden trips with destinations such as Tetulia, the Mahananda River, Banglabandha and views toward the Himalayan region.

This makes Panchagarh interesting for people who want to experience both tea culture and the natural landscape of northern Bangladesh.

10. Panchagarh Tea and Local Identity

Tea has become an important part of Panchagarh's identity.

The growth of tea cultivation has connected agriculture, local livelihoods, tourism and the image of Panchagarh as Bangladesh's northern tea-growing region.

Today, when people think about tea from northern Bangladesh, Panchagarh and Tetulia are increasingly part of that conversation.

Why Is Tetulia Important for Panchagarh Tea?

Tetulia played a central role in the early development of small-holding tea cultivation in Panchagarh.

Its location, landscape and growing tea sector have made Tetulia an important destination for understanding the development of northern Bangladesh's tea industry.

Panchagarh Tea and Kanchenjunga

Panchagarh is also known for its occasional views toward the Himalayan region, particularly around Tetulia.

On suitable clear days, visitors may combine a tea garden experience with the scenic northern landscape.

This combination of tea gardens and Himalayan scenery gives Panchagarh a distinctive identity among Bangladesh's travel destinations.

Visit the Tea Gardens of Panchagarh

Panchagarh's tea story is relatively young compared with the historic tea gardens of Sylhet and Chattogram, but its growth has been remarkable.

Suitable growing conditions, small tea growers, modern cultivation practices and continued development have helped establish Panchagarh as an important tea-growing region.

If you visit Panchagarh, exploring the tea gardens around Tetulia is one of the best ways to experience the region's modern agricultural identity.

And after exploring the gardens, enjoy a cup of authentic Panchagarh tea.
