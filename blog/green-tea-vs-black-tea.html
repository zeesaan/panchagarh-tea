Green Tea vs Black Tea: What Is the Difference?

Green tea and black tea come from the same tea plant, but they are processed differently. The biggest difference is the level of oxidation that takes place after the tea leaves are harvested.

Green tea is generally produced with little oxidation, while black tea is more extensively oxidized. This difference influences the colour, aroma and flavour of the finished tea.

Simple answer: Green tea is generally lighter and more delicate, while black tea is usually darker and stronger in flavour.

What Is Green Tea?

Green tea is made from tea leaves that undergo relatively little oxidation after harvesting.

This helps preserve a lighter colour and a flavour profile that can be fresh, vegetal, grassy or delicate depending on the tea and its processing.

Green tea can be produced in many different styles, so not every green tea tastes the same.

What Is Black Tea?

Black tea is more extensively oxidized than green tea.

This processing creates the darker appearance and stronger flavour commonly associated with black tea.

Black tea can have a wide range of flavours depending on the tea plant, growing region, leaf grade and production method.

Why Does Oxidation Matter?

Oxidation is one of the key steps that influences the character of tea after the leaves are harvested.

Green tea is processed to limit oxidation, while black tea is intentionally allowed to oxidize more extensively.

This difference contributes to the contrasting colour, aroma and flavour of the two tea styles.

Green Tea Flavour

Green tea is often associated with lighter and fresher flavours. Depending on the variety and processing method, you may notice vegetal, grassy, floral or slightly sweet characteristics.

Because green tea can be delicate, brewing temperature and time can have a noticeable effect on the final cup.

Black Tea Flavour

Black tea generally has a stronger and deeper flavour.

Depending on the tea, it may have malty, fruity, woody, spicy or robust characteristics.

Many black teas also work well with milk and sugar, although they can be enjoyed without either.

Which Tea Has More Caffeine?

Caffeine content is not determined simply by whether a tea is green or black.

The amount can vary depending on factors such as the tea variety, leaf grade, quantity used and brewing method.

For that reason, it is better not to assume that every black tea contains more caffeine than every green tea.

Tea tip:

If caffeine matters to you, check the information provided for the specific tea you are buying and consider how strongly you brew it.

Which Tea Is Easier to Drink Without Sugar?

This is mostly a matter of personal taste.

Some people enjoy the lighter character of green tea without sweetener, while others prefer the stronger flavour of black tea without sugar.

Try both styles plain before deciding which one you prefer.

Green Tea With Milk vs Black Tea With Milk

Black tea is commonly paired with milk because its stronger flavour can remain noticeable after milk is added.

Green tea is more commonly enjoyed without milk, particularly when the tea has a delicate flavour.

There is no universal rule, however. Tea is a personal experience, and people can prepare it in different ways.

How Should Green Tea Be Brewed?

Green tea is often best approached with slightly cooler water and a shorter brewing time than a strong black tea.

Very hot water or excessive brewing can make some green teas taste harsh or overly astringent.

Start with the brewing instructions provided with the specific tea and adjust according to your taste.

How Should Black Tea Be Brewed?

Black tea can generally handle hotter water and a longer infusion than many delicate green teas.

For a simple starting method, use hot water and steep the tea for around 3–4 minutes, then adjust the strength according to your preference.

For a complete brewing guide, see our article: How to Make the Perfect Cup of Black Tea .

Which Tea Is Better for Everyday Drinking?

There is no single answer.

The better choice depends on what you enjoy drinking.

  • Choose green tea if you prefer a lighter and fresher tea character.
  • Choose black tea if you prefer a stronger and fuller flavour.
  • Choose black tea if you often enjoy tea with milk.
  • Choose green tea if you usually prefer your tea without milk.
  • Try both if you are still deciding.

Does Green Tea Taste Better Than Black Tea?

Taste is personal.

Someone who enjoys delicate and fresh flavours may prefer green tea, while someone who likes a stronger cup may prefer black tea.

Instead of asking which tea is universally better, try both and compare the aroma, strength and flavour for yourself.

Can You Drink Both Green Tea and Black Tea?

Yes. Many tea drinkers enjoy different types of tea depending on the time of day, meal or personal preference.

You can enjoy a lighter green tea when you want something delicate and choose a stronger black tea when you want a more robust cup.

Green Tea vs Black Tea: The Main Difference

The biggest difference comes down to processing.

Green tea is produced with minimal oxidation, while black tea undergoes more extensive oxidation. This creates many of the differences in colour, aroma and flavour between the two.

In short: Green tea is generally lighter and more delicate. Black tea is generally darker and stronger. Both can be excellent choices depending on your taste.
